How does the House pass an uncontroversial bill in 40 minutes?

By a motion to suspend the rules, which is decided by a two-thirds vote of the members voting after 40 minutes of debate. House Rule XV clause 1(a) sets the threshold: "A rule may not be suspended except by a vote of two-thirds of the Members voting, a quorum being present." Clause 1(c) sets the debate: "A motion that the House suspend the rules is debatable for 40 minutes, one-half in favor of the motion and one-half in opposition thereto." The motion "may not be amended," and clause 1(a) restricts when the Speaker may entertain it — currently to Mondays, Tuesdays, and Wednesdays. The House Rules and Manual for the 119th Congress notes that the rule was "amended to return to Mondays, Tuesdays, and Wednesdays, but without the inclusion of the last six days of a session," reversing an every-day-allowed setting that had been in place during the 117th Congress.

How can the House force a vote on a bill leadership will not schedule?

By a discharge motion signed by a majority of the House. House Rule XV clause 2(a)(1) lets a member file a written motion to discharge (A) a committee from consideration of a public bill or public resolution "that has been referred to it for 30 legislative days" or (B) the Committee on Rules from consideration of a resolution "that has been referred to it for seven legislative days" providing a special order of business for such a bill. When "a majority of the total membership of the House" has signed the motion, it is entered on the Journal and referred to the Calendar of Motions to Discharge Committees. The Manual's annotations note that this phrase "was construed to mean 218 Members," not including Delegates or the Resident Commissioner. Under clause 2(c)(1), the motion "shall be privileged" for a call-up after at least seven legislative days on the calendar, with an end-of-session exception, and clause 2(c)(2) makes the motion "debatable for 20 minutes, one-half in favor of the motion and one-half in opposition thereto."

How is a vote actually recorded on the House floor?

Voting starts by voice; if the Speaker is in doubt or a division is demanded, the House divides, and a request for a recorded vote supported by one-fifth of a quorum is taken by electronic device with a minimum of 15 minutes for members to register. House Rule XX clause 1(a) says: "The House shall divide after the Speaker has put a question to a vote by voice as provided in clause 6 of rule I if the Speaker is in doubt or division is demanded. Those in favor of the question shall first rise or otherwise indicate from their seats and be counted, and then those opposed." Clause 1(b) adds: "If a Member, Delegate, or Resident Commissioner requests a recorded vote, and that request is supported by at least one-fifth of a quorum, the vote shall be taken by electronic device unless the Speaker invokes another procedure for recording votes provided in this rule." Clause 2(a) sets the minimum: "the minimum time for a record vote or quorum call by electronic device shall be 15 minutes."
