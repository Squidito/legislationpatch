When issued, these temporary waivers are necessary to help ensure that an adequate supply of fuel is available, particularly for emergency vehicle needs. There were no waivers issued in 2009, 2010, 2013 and 2015. This action is a renewal of the August 9, 2026, waiver extension. This action extends the ethanol blending limit in gasoline from 10 percent to 15 percent, reinstates the 1 psi allowance for ethanol blends in states where it was previously removed, and waives federal low volatility gasoline standards in two steps. In the first step, it extends the existing volatility waiver through August 31, 2026, maintaining a single national gasoline pool with 9 to 15 percent ethanol content at a common Reid Vapor Pressure standard of 10 psi. In the second step, it waives all Federal volatility standards starting on September 1, 2026, and pulls forward the wintertime volatility limits that would otherwise apply at the end of the summertime control season. The Agency has received several questions about our waiver actions: Will a transition period be provided once the waivers expire? The most recent version of EPA’s waiver actions includes language which states, “Any gasoline meeting the conditions of this waiver that was introduced into domestic pipelines or which has been certified at the fuel manufacturing facility gate or import facility , prior to the expiration date of an element of this waiver, as demonstrated by either the product transfer documents or certificates of analysis, may be distributed and sold in subject areas in the designated states until the volume identified on the PTDs or COAs is depleted. All other requirements of 40 C.F.R. Part 1090 not explicitly waived still apply for this period.” EPA intends to include similar language in subsequent waiver extensions should such extensions prove necessary. Do States need to take separate actions to effectuate the fuel waiver? They may, and many already have. States that have adopted fuel standards under consumer protection and/or air quality statutes may need to waive those provisions in State code to enable implementation of the Federal waiver in the States. We strongly encourage States to take such action as soon as possible to achieve the intent of the waiver which is to allow for an early transition to winter gasoline under the ASTM D-4814 standards. Can butane and/or pentane be blended into gasoline, including Reformulated Gasoline, under this waiver? In addition to extending the prior waiver of the butane and pentane blending requirements of 40 C.F.R. 1090.220(e), in its latest waiver action EPA is also conditionally waiving certain reporting requirements to better define and effectuate the butane and pentane blending element of our prior waiver action. EPA is conditionally waiving the testing requirement in 40 CFR 1090.1320(b)(1), and the per batch RVP reporting requirement at 40 CFR 1090.905(c)(5)(ii)(E), provided the following conditions are met. First, fuel produced under the terms of the waiver must still be reported but can be reported in batch sizes up to one batch per month. As a condition of the waiver, the certified butane blender (or certified pentane blender) must maintain an inline analyzer to monitor the blend of butane into gasoline and ensure the blended fuel meets the ASTM D_4814 volatility requirement. In addition, the certified butane blender (or certified pentane blender) must perform weekly oversight and recordkeeping to document the oversight in accordance with API 1640 Section 16.3. Lastly, where this waiver allowance is utilized, the RVP test method field on the batch report form (STR0200) should use the following text “2026 Summer RVP Waiver – ASTM D 4814 standard.” Can CBOB meeting the RVP standard under this waiver be redesignated as RBOB without retesting the fuel to demonstrate it meets the RVP limit? Yes, EPA interprets the provisions of 40 C.F.R. 1090.1010(b)(2)(iii) to allow redesignation of gasoline meeting the requirements of this waiver without recertification since the applicable RVP limit for Conventional Gasoline Blendstock for Oxygenate Blending and Reformulated Gasoline Blendstock for Oxygenate Blending and for finished conventional gasoline and reformulated gasoline are the same under the terms of the waiver action. For purposes of this waiver, distributors meet the redesignation requirement under 40 C.F.R. 1090.1010(b)(2)(v) by using the RVP value of the batch as determined by the fuel manufacturer at the refinery gate. How should refiners identify gasoline conforming to EPA Emergency Fuel Waiver from September 1 – September 15 on their EPA batch reports? Generally, from September 16, refiners indicate gasoline they sell meeting ASTM D-4814 gasoline as “winter” gasoline on EPA’s batch report form (STR0200) to indicate that no EPA RVP standard applies. Given that, we have been asked if refiners should indicate that gasoline produced from September 1 under the terms of EPA’s Emergency Fuel Waiver and meeting the September 16 ASTM D-4814 standards is “winter” gasoline in their batch reports. While the RVP standard that is being met is the winter value that would otherwise apply on September 16, we are asking refiners to identify the fuel as “summer” gasoline on the batch reports to indicate it is meeting the conditions of EPA’s waivers. Under this approach, we are asking refiners to provide the location specific limit from the ASTM D-4814 standard in the comment field of the STR0200 form. For product transfer documents (PTDs) during this period from September 1 – September 15, 2026, EPA has approved alternative PTD language for gasoline that meets the ASTM D-4814 standard. The transferor should provide the transferee the PTD with the ASTM D-4814 location specific RVP standard specified in the placeholder as: “ASTM D4814 [#.#] psi Gasoline”.
