H R.8595 · Passed House · 07-15-26

National Security, Department of State, and Related Programs Appropriations Act, 2027

Rep. Diaz-Balart, Mario (R-FL-26) · 138 pages

What does the HR 8595 do?

HR 8595 is a House bill sponsored by Rep. Mario Diaz-Balart (R-FL-26). This bill provides full-year FY2027 appropriations for the Department of State, USAID successor programs, and related national-security and foreign-assistance accounts. Title I funds the State Department and related programs. Title II funds oversight through the Offices of Inspector General. Title III funds bilateral economic assistance including global health, humanitarian relief, and development programs. Title IV funds international security assistance including foreign military financing and international military training. Title V funds contributions to multilateral development banks and the Global Environment Facility. Title VI funds the Export-Import Bank and the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation. Titles VII and VIII carry the bill's policy riders, including preconditions on assistance to the Palestinian Authority, prohibitions on funding UNRWA, the International Court of Justice, the International Criminal Court, and the Green Climate Fund, and Israel and Egypt assistance floors.

Key provisions

  • State Department Operations — $9.76B Diplomatic Programs
    • $9.76B for Diplomatic Programs, of which up to $4.16B is for Worldwide Security Protection
    • $533M from Consular and Border Security Program fees, available until expended for passport backlogs and visa wait times
    • $647M for Educational and Cultural Exchange Programs, of which not less than $287.8M is for the Fulbright Program
    • $413.6M for the Capital Investment Fund
  • Global Health, Humanitarian, and Development
    • $3.35B for Global Health Programs plus $5.53B for HIV/AIDS (PEPFAR), including a $1.25B contribution to the Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria
    • $5.0B for International Humanitarian Assistance (not less than $2.75B for disaster relief under section 491 of the Foreign Assistance Act) plus $100M for the Emergency Refugee and Migration Assistance Fund
    • $6.89B for National Security Investment Programs (not less than 15% for Africa)
    • $830M for the Millennium Challenge Corporation and $410.5M for the Peace Corps
  • Foreign Military Financing — Israel and Egypt Floors
    • $6.75B for the Foreign Military Financing Program
    • Not less than $3.30B in FMF grants for Israel, disbursed within 30 days of enactment, of which not less than $250.3M for in-Israel procurement
    • Not less than $1.30B FMF for Egypt (within a $1.425B total-assistance floor conditioned on U.S.-Egypt strategic relationship and the 1979 Egypt-Israel Peace Treaty)
    • Not less than $1.65B in total assistance for Jordan (plus an additional $400M NSIP and $50M FMF)
  • Multilateral, Export, and Development Finance
    • $504.0M contribution to the International Development Association and $139.6M to the Global Environment Facility
    • $983.25M for the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation Corporate Capital Account (net $676.45M after offsetting collections), with a $22.0B cap on total DFC loan and guarantee principal outstanding
    • $125M for Export-Import Bank administrative expenses (offset to a net $0 general-fund appropriation) plus $8.86M for the Ex-Im OIG
    • $87M for the U.S. Trade and Development Agency
  • Policy Riders — UNRWA, ICJ, ICC, WHO, Climate
    • No funds for UNRWA, the International Court of Justice, or the International Criminal Court, notwithstanding any other provision of law
    • No funds in contravention of Executive Order 14155 (Withdrawing the United States from the World Health Organization) or in support of the Pandemic Preparedness Accord until Senate ratification
    • No funds for the Green Climate Fund, Clean Technology Fund, or Loss and Damages Fund; no implementation of the Paris Agreement or a carbon tax (Sec. 7061)
    • Preconditions on assistance to a new Palestinian state (Sec. 7047), and no funds to move the U.S. Embassy in Israel away from Jerusalem (Sec. 7004(e))
