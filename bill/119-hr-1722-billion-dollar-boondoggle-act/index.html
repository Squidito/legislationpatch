What does the Billion Dollar Boondoggle Act do?

HR 1722 is a House bill sponsored by Rep. Mariannette Miller-Meeks (R-IA-1). This bill requires the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) to publish an annual report identifying federal projects that are more than five years behind schedule or at least $1B over their original cost estimate. Federal agencies must submit a defined set of information on each qualifying project — descriptions, contractors, cost histories, and any bonuses paid — so OMB can compile it for Congress and post it publicly. Creates an annual public OMB report listing federal projects that are five-plus years late or over $1B above original cost estimate, with detailed data on each.

Key provisions

  • What Triggers a Report
    • Project is more than 5 years behind its original expected completion date, or
    • Project has spent at least $1B more than its original cost estimate
    • Applies to executive agencies and independent regulatory agencies
  • Required Project Information
    • Purpose, locations, contract or award number, and start year of each project
    • Every primary contractor, subcontractor, grant recipient, and subgrantee
    • Original vs. current completion date and CPI-adjusted cost estimate
    • Amount and rationale for every award, incentive fee, or bonus paid
  • OMB Timing & Delivery
    • OMB must issue agency-reporting guidance within 1 year of enactment
    • Annual report submitted to Congress and posted on the OMB website
    • Report is unclassified but may include a classified annex
