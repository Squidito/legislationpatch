HR 7008 · Passed House · 08-06-26

Stop Insider Trading Act

Rep. Steil, Bryan (R-WI-1) · 93 cosponsors · 7 pages

What does the Stop Insider Trading Act do?

HR 7008 is a House bill sponsored by Rep. Bryan Steil (R-WI-1). This bill has two operative sections. Section 2 bars Members of Congress and their spouses and dependent children from purchasing individual stocks, requires 7-to-14-day advance public notice before selling, and imposes a fee (greater of $2,000 or ten percent of transaction value, plus any net gain) for violations. Section 3 amends the Help America Vote Act to require in-person federal-election voters to present a state, federal, or Tribal photo identification, and mail voters to submit either a photo-ID copy, their last four Social Security digits with an affidavit, or a notarization. Bans stock purchases and requires advance sale notices for Members of Congress and their spouses/dependents, and separately requires photo ID for all federal-election voting.

Key provisions

  • Congressional Stock-Trading Ban
    • Members of Congress, their spouses, and their dependent children may not purchase covered investments
    • Sales require public notice 7 to 14 calendar days before the sale, filed with the Clerk or Secretary and posted online
    • Covers securities of publicly traded companies and derivative equivalents like options and warrants
  • Exemptions from the Trading Ban
    • Excepted investment funds (as described in section 13104(f)(8)) and comparable US-, state-, or territory-concentrated funds
    • Interests in small business concerns
    • Trusts where no covered individual has authority over the trustee, and the trustee is not a Member's spouse, child, parent, or sibling
    • Spouse or dependent trades made as part of a primary occupation or employer compensation, and automatic dividend reinvestments
  • Enforcement
    • Fee equal to the greater of $2,000 or ten percent of the transaction value, plus any net gain realized
    • Fee cannot be paid from the Members' Representational Allowance, Senators' Official Personnel and Office Expense Account, or campaign contributions
    • Ethics office may refer former Members to DOJ if they resign or retire before paying
  • Federal Photo-ID Voting Requirement
    • In-person federal-election voters must present a valid physical photo identification
    • Mail voters must submit a photo-ID copy, last four SSN digits with an inability affidavit, or notarization
    • Provisional ballot available if ID missing; ID or religious-photography-objection affidavit must be produced within 3 days
  • Accepted ID Types and State Duties
    • State driver's license or ID card (with photo and expiration), U.S. passport, DoD or VA photo ID card, or Tribal-government ID with photo and expiration
    • States must provide free access to a digital imaging device (printer, copier, scanner) at courts, libraries, and police stations
    • States must notify voters of the ID requirement at registration and during online registration
